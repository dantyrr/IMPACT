About IMPACT

What is IMPACT?

IMPACT (Independent Metrics for Publication Analysis and Citation Tracking) is an open-source tool built to study publication bias, impact factor inflation, and h-index gaming in the current academic publishing system. It computes rolling 24-month journal citation rates from freely available PubMed and NIH iCite data, providing a transparent, reproducible alternative to Clarivate's proprietary Journal Impact Factor.

Key questions IMPACT helps investigate: Which journals boost their metrics with highly-cited review articles? How much does a single landmark paper or clinical guideline inflate a journal's citation rate? How do author h-indexes vary by article type, and are they padded by self-citations or review series? These questions matter for how science is evaluated, funded, and incentivized.

How is the Rolling 24-Month Citation Rate Calculated?

For each target month, IMPACT computes:

Citation Rate = Citations in the 12-month window to citable items published in the preceding 24-month window / Citable items published in that 24-month window

Citable items are research articles, reviews, case reports and guidelines. Numerator and denominator cover the same set of papers. Clarivate's Journal Impact Factor also counts citations to non-citable items such as editorials and letters in its numerator; IMPACT does not. Read the full methodology.

Data Sources

PubMed baseline and update files — ~40M records with metadata, author affiliations, and publication dates.
NIH iCite Open Citation Collection — citation links between PubMed papers, downloaded and processed locally.

Research-Only Citation Rate

IMPACT also computes a citation rate over research articles only, excluding reviews from both the numerator and denominator. The gap between the two rates shows how much of a journal's rate depends on reviews.

Accessibility & Color

All charts use the Okabe-Ito colorblind-safe palette, recommended by Nature Methods for scientific visualization. The eight core colors remain distinguishable under the most common forms of color vision deficiency — deuteranopia, protanopia, and tritanopia — which together affect roughly 8% of males and 0.5% of females. We substituted yellow and black (the original Okabe-Ito slots 7 and 8) with purple and gray for better legibility on white backgrounds.

If you have suggestions for improving accessibility, the color scheme, or any other aspect of IMPACT, please open an issue on GitHub — feedback is always welcome.

Methodology

← Back to About

The citation rate

Rate(M) = citations in the 12 months ending M, to citable items in the paper window / citable items in the paper window

Citable items are the classes research, review, case report and guideline. A citation to any other kind of item is not counted, and those items are not in the denominator. Three paper windows are computed:

VariantPaper windowPublished (months before M)
24-month (default)24 months, no gap13 to 36
12-month12 months, no gap13 to 24
5-year (years 2-6)60 months, after a 12-month gap25 to 84

Difference from Clarivate

Clarivate's numerator also counts citations to non-citable items (editorials, letters); its denominator does not. IMPACT's numerator counts only citations to citable items. IMPACT uses different data, classification and windows, and makes no comparison to Clarivate's published values.

Classifying items

PubMed's full publication-type list is read for each paper. The first match in this order sets the class: erratum or retraction notice, guideline, review (including systematic review and meta-analysis), case report, editorial, letter (Letter, Comment, Editorial Comment), other (News, Biography, Obituary, Interview, Portrait, Congress), and finally research (Journal Article).

A paper PubMed types only as a journal article, with no abstract, is reclassified as commentary (not citable) if it has two pages or fewer, or if iCite says it is not a research article. iCite's flag is evidence for this rule only; it does not set the research class by itself.

Items PubMed types as "Letter" are not counted as citable. This undercounts short research formats such as research letters at journals that publish them under that type.

Reliability flags

Computed on the latest month whose 12-month citation window ended at least 12 months ago: fewer than 20 research items; fewer than 15 review items; reviews at least 50% of citable items; at least 10% of papers with an inferred class.

Dates and citation months

A paper is dated by its electronic publication date when PubMed records one, otherwise its issue date; a missing month is set to June. iCite gives only the year of each citation, so the month is taken from the citing paper's PubMed record.

Authors and geography

Before about 2014, PubMed recorded only the first author's affiliation, so last-author and country counts for earlier years are incomplete. One affiliation parser now produces all countries, so Geography values may differ from earlier releases.
